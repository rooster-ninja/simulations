Simulations

Small interactive simulations. Each one runs entirely in the browser.

  • Moon, Earth's shadow and the gegenschein

    Watch the Moon lap Earth, cross its nodes, and slip into (or just miss) Earth's shadow. Lunar eclipses 2026–2030.

  • Seestar S30 star plate: how big is an arcsecond?

    Zoom a simulated frame from the full 2.1° field down to single 4″ pixels, with an adaptive arcsecond scale bar and Barnard's Star's parallax for comparison.

  • Plumb line curvature test

    A null-hypothesis field test for surface curvature: zenith star cameras plus radio time-of-flight ranging. Equipment, diagrams, procedure, decision rule and a test-planning calculator.

  • Plumb line test review

    What the zenith test measures and outputs, its link to celestial navigation (1′ = 1 NM), and why a positive result does not rule out a flat-plus-dome model.

  • Disc model quasar test (WIP)

    Work in progress: a stationary disc with two turning star fields, tested against 30 real quasars seen from 24 cities, with every star given free position, height, speed and launch angle. Red error bars show the misses.

  • Celestial theodolite: planar prediction

    Star occultations behind mountain peaks on a flat plane. θ₁ from the peak–observer triangle, θ₂ with the observer on the peak, and the recorded ♈︎ shift, for every logged peak.

  • Triangulation chain: baseline to check base

    A bar-measured 10 km baseline carried through seven first-order triangles to a check base. Each triangle is shaded by spherical excess, R is estimated from the angles alone, and a malice section shows which checks catch fudged data.

  • Peak chord curvature

    Single straight Giga-Ranger chords between Nanton, Mount Burke and Plateau Mountain, plus a centre station, give a radius of curvature with error bars. Three chords fit any surface; the fourth station decides. Tap the map to place it.

  • Deflection of the vertical: US chains

    Every observed angle of the 39th-parallel and Eastern Oblique chains corrected for the local plumb line with NGS DEFLEC18 and Schott's station heights, then refitted. The chains on the curvature dome, coloured by how much each triangle's excess moves.

  • Shasta · Lola · Helena

    The US Coast Survey's largest triangle (1879). Its 306.5 km Shasta–Helena side drawn to true scale against a flat chord, with a vertical-stretch slider: the surface bulges 1 842 ± 29 m above the chord. Angle sum 180° 02′ 22.7″.

  • Flat plane, lumpy triangles

    The 39th-parallel and Eastern Oblique chains on a flat map with no curve fitted. Each triangle tested on its own against a flat triangle, its excess drawn as a lump. Triangles with an unobserved angle in red; every break in the chains pinned.
