Flat plane, lumpy triangles

Each triangle of the two US chains is laid on a flat map and tested on its own: do its three observed angles add up to 180°, as a flat triangle must? Its measured excess is drawn as a lump over its own footprint. Nothing is fitted under the chain as a whole. Triangles with an angle that was never observed are red, and every place the chain is broken is flagged.

2 · One triangle at a time, against a flat triangle
  1. Observed excess. The three station-adjusted angles printed by Schott, before the network adjustment, minus 180°. A flat triangle gives 0.
  2. Noise. The survey's closure noise per triangle: ±1.261″ for the 39th parallel, ±1.397″ for the Eastern Oblique.
  3. Test. z = observed excess ÷ noise. On a flat plane z scatters around 0 with spread 1, with as many negative as positive.
  4. Unresolved. If Schott's table shows an angle as "…." the angle was not observed, the triangle's sum closes by construction, and it is not tested. It is drawn red and gets no lump.
  5. No surface. No radius or curve is fitted to the chain. The ellipsoid excess is shown only for comparison.
z = (A + B + C − 180°) / σclosure · own radius = √(area / excess)

Finding: of 716 triangles tested on their own, 628 have angles summing to more than 180° and 88 to less; 220 exceed a flat triangle by more than 3σ, and the largest by 60σ. A flat plane predicts the red curve. The negative triangles are all small (under 747 km²) and none falls below -3.0σ, as noise around a small positive excess would give. Small triangles have an expected excess below the noise under any model, so they pile up near zero; the test lives in the large triangles of the tail. This is an inductive result from Schott's printed angles and the stated noise.

5 · Where the chains break

No break is caused by an unresolved triangle. Every red triangle sits inside a net whose resolved triangles still join each other by shared sides around it: removing all 30 leaves the same segments (11 and 6). The red triangles are holes in the test, not cuts in the chain.

The chains do break in 15 other places in this transcription: 6 joins pass through a single station with no shared side, so no triangle carries a side across; 9 are gaps where no triangle in the data set joins the two parts. Gaps may be sections the report did not transcribe rather than gaps in Schott's network; they are listed for checking against the printed tables.

7 · What lumps on a flat plane would have to do

The lumps are a picture of each triangle's measured excess, not a surface that could produce it. By Gauss–Bonnet, a bump that rises from flat ground and returns to it encloses zero net curvature: its positive cap is paid for by a negative skirt. A triangle drawn around one whole bump would show zero excess, and the triangles over the skirt would show negative excess.

A flat plane with lumps therefore predicts excesses that cancel in the sum over neighbouring triangles. In the chain tiles they do not: every segment above sums positive, and segment 1 of the 39th parallel sums 1269″ over 46 tiles, 148σ above flat.
