Theorema Egregium · Hanoverian arc measurement

Building Gauss's Great Triangle

From a 5.9 km line of rods at Braak to the triangle Hohehagen – Brocken – Inselsberg, one step at a time.

Station positions are approximate modern locations and the chain is a reconstruction. Gauss's network had more stations and check diagonals.

Braak base, 1820: 5 875.219 m, measured with calibrated 4 m rods under H. C. Schumacher; Gauss took part. — vermessungsgeschichte.de, Dänische Basismessung in Braak 1820; Hannoversche Gradmessung 1821–1823 (12-inch Reichenbach/Ertel theodolite, heliotropes, Göttingen–Altona arc).

Observed angles Hohehagen 86°13′58.366″, Brocken 53°6′45.642″, Inselsberg 40°39′30.165″, sum 180°0′14.173″ — as quoted from Gauss in the Berkeley Physics Course, vol. 1 (Kittel et al.), ch. 1. Breitenberger (1984), reconstructing from Gauss's data, gives the sum as 180°0′14.211″ and the closure after reduction as −0.642″ — cited in E. Scholz, C.F. Gauß' Präzisionsmessungen terrestrischer Dreiecke.

Excess 14.85348″ and reductions Hohehagen −4.95113″, Brocken −4.95104″, Inselsberg −4.95131″ — Gauss, Disquisitiones generales circa superficies curvas (1827), quoted in arXiv:2208.12757.

Side Inselsberg–Brocken 105 972.85 m and latitudes Inselsberg 50°51′9″, Hohehagen 51°28′31″, Brocken 51°48′2″ — W. Jordan, Handbuch der Vermessungskunde (1896), worked example on Gauss's triangle. Latitudes Göttingen 51°31′47.85″, Altona 53°32′45.27″ — Jordan (1895), citing Gauss, Bestimmung des Breitenunterschiedes (1828).

Station heights Brocken 1156 m, Hohehagen 508 m, Inselsberg 916 m; mean error of a net direction 0.48″ (Gauss to Bessel, 1823) — Scholz, citing Breitenberger (1984) and Gauss Werke IX, 366.

Deflection of the vertical on the Brocken: Gauss's geodetic latitude differed from von Zach's astronomical latitude by about 10″ — Brosche & Heckmann, zfv 2016.

Chain stations (Braak, Hamburg, Hohenhorn, Lüneburg, Nindorf, Wilsede, Wulfsode, Hauselberg, Falkenberg, Garssen, Deister, Lichtenberg, Hils, Brocken, Hohehagen, Göttingen, Inselsberg) from Jordan (1895) Fig. 3; sight lines Deister → Hils, Lichtenberg, Garssen, Falkenberg (myheimat.de); triangles Hauselberg–Wulfsode–Wilsede and Wulfsode–Hauselberg–Falkenberg (Lüneburg Heath histories). The order of the other chain triangles and all station positions here are approximate reconstructions, not Gauss's data.

1 · Methods

Steps 1–8 trace how a length grows from the Braak base down the chain by the law of sines, b = a sin B / sin A. The chain stations are real, but their positions are approximate modern locations and the triangle order is a reconstruction; those lengths are illustrative only.

Steps 9–13 use Gauss's own numbers for the great triangle: the observed angles, his computed excess and per-vertex reductions, and Jordan's side Inselsberg–Brocken. The plane angles are the observed angles minus Gauss's reductions, with the closing error split evenly. The other sides follow by the law of sines, the area as ½ · b · c · sin A, and the implied radius as R = √(area / observed excess).
