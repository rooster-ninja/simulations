An Experimental Inquiry

On the Time of Light-Signals in the Telegraph Fibres, and What It Reveals of the Figure of the Earth

Communicated from rooster.ninja · Observations of 12 November 2026
❦ ❦ ❦

Abstract

§ I.Of the Principle

No message can outrun light. A signal sent through a glass fibre travels slower still, at the speed of light divided by the group index of the glass, which for ordinary telegraph fibre is near 1.468, or about two-thirds of the speed of light. Routers, queues and the winding of cables can only add time to the journey; nothing on the path can take time away. Hence the shortest round trip ever measured between two places sets a ceiling on how far apart they can be, and any proposed map of the Earth that puts them farther apart than that ceiling must be wrong.

For a pair of stations separated by a distance d, the round-trip floor through fibre, and the absolute floor in a vacuum, are

tfib = 2d ng / c,    tvac = 2d / c(1)

We compute d twice for every pair, each time as the shortest path the model allows. On the globe it is the great-circle arc along the surface of a sphere of 6,371 km. On the flat map, the north-pole azimuthal equidistant projection, a place at latitude φ lies at radius r = (90° − φ) × 111.195 km from the pole, and d is the straight line between the two points:

dflat2 = r12 + r22 − 2r1r2 cos Δλ(2)

No cable on a plane can be shorter than that straight line, so the flat-map floor is the most favourable that map can claim. A measured minimum falling below a model’s fibre floor rules that model out for that pair. A minimum above both floors decides nothing, for slow routes are allowed under either figure.

§ II.Of the Design of the Experiment, and the Reasons for Each Choice

  1. Minimum, not mean. Every delay on the path is positive, so the fastest of sixteen echoes is the one closest to the physical floor. The mean measures congestion; the minimum measures geometry.
  2. Single-site target servers. Each target is a Vultr test host in one named datacentre. Large services answer from many places at once under one address (“anycast”), which would let a nearby machine reply for a distant city and fake a short time. A single-site host removes that trap.
  3. Three probes on different networks. Each source city pings each target from up to three Globalping probes run by different internet providers. Each provider takes its own route, and only the shortest route tests the floor, so the lowest of the three is kept.
  4. A traceroute from the same probes. The hop list shows where the path really went, through a direct cable or round by Los Angeles and London, and confirms that the last hop is the labelled datacentre.
  5. A “home check” on every server’s position. Each target is also pinged from probes in its own city. A minimum under 6 ms places it within a few hundred kilometres of that city. At such short range the globe and the flat map agree to well under one part in a hundred, so this check fixes the server’s position without assuming either figure of the Earth. Locating servers from long-distance pings would be circular, since turning a time into a distance already presumes a map.
  6. The same fibre floor for both figures. The signals travel in glass: the routes are submarine and terrestrial fibre, and the traceroutes show no satellite hops. So both models are held to the same standard, the speed of light in fibre over that model’s shortest path, taking the slower index of 1.468 that gives the lower floor. The vacuum floor, light at full speed with no glass at all, is shown as well as an absolute limit. A model broken below its vacuum floor could not be saved by any medium; one broken below only its fibre floor could be saved only if part of the route ran outside glass, by radio or laser link.
  7. A northern source first, a southern source second. From Vancouver the flat map stretches distances by only 0–14 %, less than ordinary route slack, so this series is expected to decide nothing and serves as the calibration. From Santiago the flat map stretches distances by up to two and a third times, and the two figures part company.
  8. Clocks only at the sender. The round trip is timed by one clock at the probe, so its absolute setting cancels. Only clock error can make a reading too fast, and that is fixed at 0.05 ms (§ III). A companion pinger that never puts the time inside the packet, and authenticates every reply with a secret-keyed hash, has been written for stations under our own control.

§ III.Of the Errors

Three errors enter. Position: probes and datacentres are known to city level, taken as 25 km (1σ) at each end. This was carried through equations (1) and (2) by 4,000 Monte Carlo trials per pair, giving a floor uncertainty of 0.34–0.80 ms. Timing: 0.05 ms on the fast side, the only side that matters, since path delays can only lengthen a ping. Glass: the group index of commercial fibre lies between 1.462 and 1.468; using 1.468 gives the higher floor, but the verdicts are the same at 1.462.

The headroom of a pair is its minimum ping less the floor, and its significance is that headroom divided by the combined uncertainty, σ = √(σfloor2 + 0.052). A negative headroom of several σ rules the model out for that pair. The error bars are drawn at ±1σ (heavy) and ±3σ (light). The combined σ is only about 0.35–0.8 ms, under one part in two hundred of a 200 ms round trip, so on the full-scale Plates I–III the bars, drawn true to scale, are barely wider than the marks. Plate IV shows the closest cases on an axis narrow enough to read them. Their smallness is itself a finding: a city-level position error cannot move any floor by more than about a millisecond.

§ VII.Conclusions

Three limits remain. Only one flat layout, the north-pole azimuthal equidistant projection, is tested; other flat arrangements would need their own distance formula. The São Paulo result rests on one pair and should be repeated from several networks and on other days. And the strongest southern pairs, to Sydney, Melbourne and Johannesburg, will decide nothing until a route runs direct.

§ VIII.Of Repeating the Experiment

The apparatus is two Python scripts using only the standard library, kept in this page’s scripts folder with instructions in its README. coastal_campaign.py runs a whole series from any computer through the Globalping network, choosing the source city on the command line. verified_ping.py is the companion pinger for a station of one’s own (Linux, run as root); it times each echo from kernel timestamps at the sender, and accepts a reply only if its random, hash-sealed payload returns intact.

python3 coastal_campaign.py Santiago CL
